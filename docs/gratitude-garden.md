# Paca’s Gratitude Garden

The garden lives at `/paca-village/holiday/gratitude-garden/` in the existing portfolio. The gingerbread cottage is the ninth stop on `/paca-village/` and opens Paca’s Holiday Village at `/paca-village/holiday/`. Its garden illustration, sign, and invitation form one accessible link into the gratitude garden. Panchita’s scarf logo sits above the garden title. Small Paca Village and Home links provide a way back, and the footer returns to the holiday village. Shared gratitude links and metadata use the garden’s own URL. The portfolio is published through its normal GitHub Pages deployment.

The separate notes service is deployed at https://paca-holiday-village.courtneythko.chatgpt.site. Public anonymous participation was explicitly approved. The keeper page at `/keeper` requires the configured owner's ChatGPT account. Pending notes never enter the public API. The portfolio uses that hosted service by default; set `NEXT_PUBLIC_PACA_GARDEN_API_URL=http://127.0.0.1:5180` when developing against its local database. The notes service is hosted and deployed separately from this static portfolio.

Implemented: watercolor garden, tappable tree leaves, five selectable styles, anonymous messages and optional names, moderated shared storage, hearts, approved community count, mobile note sheets, planting animation, PNG postcard export, pause/reduced motion, writing prompts, live tree growth, decorative milestone unlocks, milestone previews and Christmas teaser. GMI-generated drifting leaves and a planting twirl are included. Page-wide windblown maple, oak and sage leaves sweep across every section, with varied depth, tumbling and synchronized gusts. The Christmas wishing experience is future work. Eight welcome notes are labeled as Paca's notes and do not inflate the community count.

Validation: TypeScript; full Next.js production export using Webpack; backend production build; local API integration tests including concurrent rate limiting; browser planting, reaction and postcard download; mobile 390px viewport with no horizontal overflow and a bottom sheet note reader. The default Turbopack production build stalled in this environment; `npm run build -- --webpack` completed successfully.

The TypeScript include paths now target `src/` so the portfolio no longer attempts to compile unrelated nested apps under `output/` and `sites/`.

## Community growth

The garden now begins with mostly bare branches and fills with layered watercolor foliage as the approved community count rises. Two matching full-scene paintings blend progressively from `gratitude-garden-beginning.png` to `gratitude-garden-flourishing.png`; the canopy reaches full density at 250 notes while the real counter continues growing. The original `gratitude-garden.png` is preserved for the village invitation and postcards. The old enlarged duplicate tree and flat decorative leaf overlay have been replaced with the painted foliage.

Milestones add pumpkins at 3, a bench at 10, fairy lights at 25, small alpaca friends at 50, a fountain at 100, and a golden glow at 250. These smaller goals let a new community unlock decorations sooner. Stage labels, previews, progress and the tree’s growth curve share these thresholds. Growth uses the API's `growthTotal`: approved public notes plus personal cards first opened from another browser identity. The API retains `total` for approved notes and adds `sharedCards` for opened cards; the garden shows both in a breakdown. Welcome notes, pending notes, unopened cards, sender previews and repeated opens do not add leaves. The visible page checks for updates every 30 seconds and on returning to the tab. Paused and reduced-motion modes show the same growth without animations.

At 50 notes, three distinct transparent watercolor alpacas join the two original painted alpacas. Their sprite sheet, `alpaca-friends-watercolor.png`, replaces the two small outlined SVG animals. All three new PNGs were made with the built-in image_gen tool; exact prompts and saved source paths are recorded in `docs/garden-watercolor-refresh.json`. Their transparent alpha is retained, and the original garden illustration and Panchita logo are unchanged.

The counter labels gratitude leaves, including zero, and separates approved public notes from opened personal cards. The public form states that submissions need approval; its saved confirmation says “awaiting review,” explains that approval adds one to the counter, and reassures visitors not to resubmit. Owners review notes at the notes service’s `/keeper` page. Personal card text never enters that queue or the public notes feed.

Milestone buttons provide a clearly labeled visual preview without changing the real count or writing any records. “Back to our garden” returns to the live count. `node --test tests/garden-growth.test.cjs` checks growth boundaries, invalid/loading counts, stable positions, and large-community limits.

## Personal thank-you cards

The sender enters only the recipient's name on the site; their email address is entered in the email app. After preparing a card, the browser renders a PNG attachment in advance and shows its actual image. “Email postcard image” uses `navigator.share({ files, title, text })`; the sender chooses Mail or another email app from the device's share menu, adds the address and sends there. The text includes the full card link and growth receipt. The image is rendered before the click to preserve transient user activation, and editing the card invalidates the old attachment. Object URLs are revoked when replaced or unmounted. Neither the image nor email addresses are uploaded to the notes service.

File support is checked with `navigator.canShare({ files: [file] })`, separately from link sharing. Unsupported browsers show “Download postcard for email” and explicit manual-attachment instructions, plus an optional email draft with no recipient. `mailto:` is only that fallback and cannot attach a file. Share cancellation and rendering failures do not claim delivery; failed rendering can be retried, and link sharing remains available. The site cannot guarantee a particular email app is installed or that it retains the shared text, so senders are reminded to keep the card link with the image. Only opening that link counts toward tree growth; viewing the attached PNG alone does not. Tests cover PNG file payloads, Unicode and growth links, subject encoding, blank recipients, and file-sharing capability/failure fallbacks. Implementation follows the [Web Share specification](https://w3c.github.io/web-share/) and [WebKit's user-activation guidance](https://webkit.org/blog/13862/the-user-activation-api/).

The garden's gratitude panel has “Plant gratitude” and “Send to someone” tabs. Each draft survives switching tabs, with arrow keys and Home/End supported. The sending tab embeds the same composer available at `/paca-village/holiday/gratitude-garden/thanks/`. Visitors choose a leaf, enter a recipient and message, optionally sign their name, and preview a watercolor card. They can use their device's share sheet, copy its link into a text or DM, or download a personalized PNG. Recipients can save their keepsake or create a thank-you of their own. The site does not send messages or claim delivery.

The versioned, validated card payload lives in the link's URL fragment. It is not encrypted: anyone with the complete link can read it. Its names and message are not stored in the notes service or displayed in the public garden. Shared links use the production domain; the preview link stays on the current host for local testing. Analytics scripts are excluded from the standalone composer and recipient page, which also use `noindex`, `no-referrer`, and generic link-preview metadata without the personal message. Invalid or incomplete links show a recovery message.

New cards register an anonymous contribution receipt through `POST /api/cards`, sending only a SHA-256 content fingerprint, form timing, honeypot and browser visitor identifier. The service stores a random receipt ID, a digest of the sender identity plus fingerprint for retry deduplication, a hashed sender identity, creation time and optional first-open time. It never receives the card text. The link includes the receipt as an optional `growthId`; older links remain readable but do not retroactively contribute. A valid `POST /api/cards/open` from another browser identity fills the first-open timestamp once, using a conditional update that also handles simultaneous opens. Sender previews are excluded. This is anonymous browser-based deduplication, not verified recipient identity; changing browsers or clearing storage can change the identity. Registration is limited to five new cards per IP per hour, and first opens to thirty per IP per hour. Card creation remains usable if the service fails, with a retry connection option; recipients can retry a failed growth update. A downloaded image alone cannot report that it was opened.

The separate Sites backend adds the schema-only migration `0001_romantic_zombie.sql` for `gratitude_cards`. Its local-only `tests/cards.integration.mjs` checks origin and input validation, registration retries, sender exclusion, concurrent first opens, aggregate counts, public-feed isolation, rate limits, and pending-note moderation. All write tests use an isolated local database, not production.

`node --test tests/gratitude-card.test.cjs tests/garden-growth.test.cjs` covers Unicode round trips, input limits, malformed links, allowed leaf colors, production URLs, and growth behavior. Browser checks cover composing, copying a link, opening the recipient view, downloading its PNG, mobile recipient width, and the absence of analytics scripts on card pages. The production export includes the new static route.

## Seasonal media

Generated with the GMI Cloud Tools plugin after approval of the $0.87 quote: two PixVerse v6 8-second 720p clips ($0.36 each), and one MiniMax Music 3.0 track ($0.15). Exact prompts, settings, request IDs and source URLs are in `docs/garden-media-provenance.json`.

- `public/holiday/autumn-leaf-drift.mp4`: muted ambient loop over the original painting; a lightening filter and multiply blending remove the generated light backdrop. Existing SVG falling leaves remain the loading/error fallback.
- `public/holiday/gratitude-leaf-twirl.mp4`: one-shot flourish after planting or previewing a growth milestone.
- Earlier music versions are rejected. The original GMI “Little Thanks” contained vocals; the vocal-separated preview lacked melody, and the later synthesized-piano edit was rejected as messy. All three remain under `output/holiday/` rather than public assets.
- The user approved an additional $0.15 for a fresh MiniMax Music 3.0 song with the documented `[Inst]` structure tag and a simpler piano-led arrangement. GMI returned 58.41 seconds, despite the prompt asking for about two minutes. The raw remake is `output/holiday/autumn-lofi-gmi-v2.mp3`. The user confirmed audible vocals in this remake and rejected it. Do not use or present this track as an instrumental. Automatic speech checks were insufficient to verify the explicit no-vocals requirement. The exact request and result are recorded in the media provenance file. Automatic screening cannot prove the absence of singing or humming.
- The current player uses `public/holiday/autumn-lofi-instrumental.mp3`, a new MiniMax Music 3.0 request with the native `is_instrumental: true` flag and `lyrics_optimizer: false`, approved for another $0.15. The required GMI lyrics field contains only `[Inst]`. MiniMax returned a 17.21-second clip; the player loops it, starts only on a click, and defaults to 25% volume. This is raw generated audio with no added melody or local remix. Automatic AudioSet AST checks found no strong singing or humming signal, while detecting humming in the previously rejected remake; speech recognition produced a low-confidence opening result. These checks are not a listening guarantee. See [MiniMax’s instrumental-mode documentation](https://platform.minimax.io/docs/api-reference/music-generation) and the exact request in `output/holiday/gmi-instrumental-request.json`.

The leaf videos and page-wide weather share the pause control. The weather layer uses 48 leaves on desktop and 30 on mobile, never intercepts input, and is hidden for reduced-motion preferences. The original garden PNG is unchanged.
